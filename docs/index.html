1 Project Overview

This book documents the reproducible workflow for a global comparative analysis of environmental variation and extra-pair paternity (EPP) in birds.

The project focuses on annual, population-level estimates of EPP in socially monogamous bird species. Each record represents a population-level estimate of the proportion of broods containing at least one extra-pair young in a defined study year or study period, linked to geographic coordinates, breeding-season timing, environmental variables, and phylogenetic information.

TipMain Aim

The main aim is to test whether spatial and temporal variation in EPP is associated with climatic conditions, climatic variability, habitat productivity, and environmental anomalies.

1.1 Scope of the Dataset

The analysis is restricted to records that meet the criteria below. These rules keep the dataset comparable across species, populations, and study systems.

ImportantInclusion Criteria
  • The species is classified as socially monogamous.
  • The EPP estimate is available for a known year or study period.
  • The number of sampled broods and the number of broods with EPP are known.
  • Geographic coordinates are available or can be assigned reliably.
  • The breeding season can be linked to monthly environmental data.
  • The species can be matched to the taxonomic and phylogenetic backbone used in the analysis.

1.2 Predictor Groups

Environmental predictors are organized into four broad groups.

1.2.1 Mean Climatic Conditions

Seasonal or monthly temperature and precipitation values linked to each EPP record.

1.2.2 Climatic Variability

Measures such as diurnal temperature range or within-season variation in temperature and precipitation.

1.2.3 Environmental Anomalies

Short-term and long-term deviations from local climatic baselines.

1.2.4 Habitat Productivity

NDVI-based measures of vegetation productivity during the relevant breeding-season window.

1.3 Additional Covariates

The models also include methodological, geographic, and biological covariates where appropriate.

NoteCovariates Considered in the Models
  • Marker type
  • Nest box use
  • Absolute latitude
  • Distance to coast
  • Species identity
  • Population identity
  • Phylogenetic relatedness among species

1.4 Workflow

The book is organized as a reproducible research workflow, moving from raw data preparation to analytical outputs.

  1. Data preparation - cleaning the raw EPP dataset and checking response variables, breeding-system categories, coordinates, dates, and sampling effort.
  2. Taxonomic matching and phylogeny - matching species names to the taxonomic backbone and preparing the phylogenetic covariance matrix.
  3. Environmental variables - linking each EPP record to climate and NDVI data using location, year, and breeding-season timing.
  4. Exploratory analyses - summarising sample sizes, missing data, predictor distributions, and correlations among environmental variables.
  5. Overall model diagnostics - evaluating the random-effect structure used in the EPP models.
  6. Environmental models - fitting beta-binomial models of EPP while accounting for population, species, and phylogenetic non-independence.
  7. Figures - producing model summaries, posterior predictions, and manuscript-ready figures.

1.5 Key Terms

Extra-pair paternity (EPP)
The occurrence of offspring within a socially monogamous brood that are sired by a male other than the social father.

Population-level estimate
An EPP estimate associated with a particular population, study site, year, or study period.

Environmental anomaly
A deviation in environmental conditions from a relevant long-term baseline.

CautionReproducibility Principle

Every analytical decision should be traceable from raw input data to final figures, tables, and model outputs.
