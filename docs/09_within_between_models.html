9.1 Overview

The main anomaly models estimate one coefficient for each climate anomaly. That coefficient combines two biologically different comparisons: differences in the average anomaly among populations and departures from a population’s own average across its repeated records. This chapter restores the prespecified within-between distinction from objective (4). It asks whether extra-pair paternity (EPP) is associated with climate differences among populations, with temporal departures within the same population, or with both.

This is a pair of confirmatory models: the short temperature anomaly is decomposed in one model and the long temperature anomaly in the other. The matching precipitation anomaly remains an unsplit adjustment covariate. DTR, NDVI, and the remaining environmental predictors are not decomposed because they are outside this specific hypothesis; DTR-anomaly analysis is a separate task.

9.2 Information available for the within-population effect

The exact fitting frame contains 441 records from 171 populations. Of these populations, 102 (59.6%) have at least two records. The 69 singleton populations have a within-population deviation of exactly zero: they inform the between-population effect and the other model terms, but provide no information about the within-population slope. Thus, the between effect uses 171 populations, whereas the within effect is effectively estimated from 102 populations, and from 101 populations with genuinely nonzero temperature-anomaly variation.

9.3 Within-population centring

Following the within-subject-centring definition of van de Pol and Wright (2009), adapted here from subjects to populations, for temperature anomaly \(x_{ij}\) in record \(i\) from population \(j\) the script constructs the population mean \(\bar{x}_j\) and the within-population departure \(x_{ij}-\bar{x}_j\). Both components use the centre and standard deviation of the original anomaly on the complete 441-row model frame:

\[ x^{between}_{ij} = \frac{\bar{x}_j - \bar{x}}{s_x}, \qquad x^{within}_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - \bar{x}_j}{s_x}. \]

Here, \(\bar{x}_j\) is explicitly the population mean of the temperature anomaly, not the mean of raw or baseline temperature. The source-variable audit below stops the script if a raw-temperature or baseline-temperature column is substituted. Using the same scale for both components makes the posterior contrast \(\beta_{within}-\beta_{between}\) meaningful.

9.7 Anomaly effects

The coefficients below are on the same one-standard-deviation scale as the original anomaly variables. The between-population term describes differences among population means; the within-population term describes departures from the same population’s own mean.

## Overview

The main anomaly models estimate one coefficient for each climate anomaly. That coefficient combines two biologically different comparisons: differences in the average anomaly among populations and departures from a population's own average across its repeated records. This chapter restores the prespecified within-between distinction from objective (4). It asks whether extra-pair paternity (EPP) is associated with climate differences among populations, with temporal departures within the same population, or with both.

This is a pair of confirmatory models: the short temperature anomaly is decomposed in one model and the long temperature anomaly in the other. The matching precipitation anomaly remains an unsplit adjustment covariate. DTR, NDVI, and the remaining environmental predictors are not decomposed because they are outside this specific hypothesis; DTR-anomaly analysis is a separate task.

## Within-population centring

Following the within-subject-centring definition of [van de Pol and Wright (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2008.11.006), adapted here from subjects to populations, for temperature anomaly $x_{ij}$ in record $i$ from population $j$ the script constructs the population mean $\bar{x}_j$ and the within-population departure $x_{ij}-\bar{x}_j$. Both components use the centre and standard deviation of the original anomaly on the complete 441-row model frame:

$$
x^{between}_{ij} = \frac{\bar{x}_j - \bar{x}}{s_x}, \qquad
x^{within}_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - \bar{x}_j}{s_x}.
$$

Here, $\bar{x}_j$ is explicitly the population mean of the **temperature anomaly**, not the mean of raw or baseline temperature. The source-variable audit below stops the script if a raw-temperature or baseline-temperature column is substituted. Using the same scale for both components makes the posterior contrast $\beta_{within}-\beta_{between}$ meaningful.

```{r}

```

The identity $x^z_{ij}=x^{between}_{ij}+x^{within}_{ij}$ is only a coding sanity check, not a test of identifiability. The script also checks that within deviations sum to zero by population and that the literal reconstruction of `priors_mu` matches the main-model definition.

    n_epp_broods | trials(n_broods_sampled) ~
      tmp_short_anom_between_z + tmp_short_anom_within_z +
