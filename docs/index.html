Thisdocuments a reproducible comparative analysis of environmental variation and extra-pair paternity (EPP) in birds. The analysis uses population-level estimates of the proportion of sampled broods containing at least one extra-pair young.

Project overview

Comparative analysis of population-level extra-pair paternity in birds, linked to sampling effort, population identity, species identity, phylogeny, location, breeding-season timing, and environmental data.

Main question

Do population-level EPP rates vary along broad environmental gradients related to temperature, precipitation, thermal variability, productivity, latitude, and distance to coast?

2
∴
Analytical strategy

Six environmental and geographic variables are summarised using PCA, producing two broad environmental-geographic axes.

PCA of six variables
PC1: thermal-geographic gradient

warmer, more thermally variable, lower-latitude, more inland environments

PC2: wet-productivity gradient

wetter and more productive environments

Model roadmap

The model set focuses on full environmental models with mean-only and mean-scale versions, supported by direct-predictor sensitivity analyses.

A
Main models

Three full environmental model groups: environmental PCA, short anomaly, and long anomaly.

mean-only and mean-scale beta-binomial models

Methodological controls are included in the full environmental models and evaluated again in sensitivity models.

B
Scale component

Each hypothesis group is fitted with a constant precision model and a scale model.

mean model + optional phi model

The scale component tests whether predictors are associated with residual precision and overdispersion.

C
Sensitivity models

Direct environmental-geographic predictors replace PCA axes and anomaly-only formulations.

plus nest_box and marker_type

These models check whether conclusions depend on modelling choices and methodological controls.

⊞
Core model structure

All Bayesian models use the same response and repeated-observation structure.

Response:
broods with EPP / total broods sampled
Likelihood:
beta-binomial
Random effects:
population identity, species identity, phylogenetic relatedness
(1 | population_id) + (1 | species_nonphylo) + (1 | gr(species_phylo, cov = phylo_mat))

0.1 Analytical strategy

The main analysis is organised around three full environmental model groups. These models test whether broad environmental-geographic gradients, short-term climate anomalies, and long-term climate anomalies are associated with population-level EPP. Each group is fitted as a mean-only beta-binomial mixed model and as a mean-scale model, with methodological controls included in the model set.

The environmental PCA summarises six environmental and geographic variables. The two primary axes are interpreted as PC1, a thermal-geographic gradient, and PC2, a wet-productivity gradient.

0.2 Model roadmap

The main environmental models chapter presents the three primary hypothesis groups: an environmental PCA model, a short-anomaly model, and a long-anomaly model. Each group is fitted with and without a scale component.

The scale component asks whether environmental predictors and methodological controls are associated not only with mean EPP, but also with residual precision and overdispersion in EPP.

The sensitivity models chapter uses environmental-geographic predictors directly, rather than PCA axes or anomaly-only models

0.3 Reproducibility

The workflow first documents data preparation and phylogenetic matching, then describes the common model structure, constructs the environmental PCA, fits the full environmental models, evaluates the scale component, and presents sensitivity analyses and figures.

## Analytical strategy

The main analysis is organised around three full environmental model groups. These models test whether broad environmental-geographic gradients, short-term climate anomalies, and long-term climate anomalies are associated with population-level EPP. Each group is fitted as a mean-only beta-binomial mixed model and as a mean-scale model, with methodological controls included in the model set.

The environmental PCA summarises six environmental and geographic variables. The two primary axes are interpreted as PC1, a thermal-geographic gradient, and PC2, a wet-productivity gradient.

## Model roadmap

The **main environmental models** chapter presents the three primary hypothesis groups: an environmental PCA model, a short-anomaly model, and a long-anomaly model. Each group is fitted with and without a scale component.

The scale component asks whether environmental predictors and methodological controls are associated not only with mean EPP, but also with residual precision and overdispersion in EPP.

The **sensitivity models** chapter uses environmental-geographic predictors directly, rather than PCA axes or anomaly-only models

## Reproducibility

The workflow first documents data preparation and phylogenetic matching, then describes the common model structure, constructs the environmental PCA, fits the full environmental models, evaluates the scale component, and presents sensitivity analyses and figures.
