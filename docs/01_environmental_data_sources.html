1.1 Overview

The large CRU NetCDF archives are not redistributed in this repository.

WarningRaw-climate provenance limitation

The original repository contained the derived CRU columns but did not contain the post-1990 monthly NetCDF inputs, the original extraction script, or the original locality-to-cell lookup. It was therefore not self-contained at the raw-climate level. CRU TS v4.09 and the used grid cells were identified later by exact empirical reconstruction of the stored values. This demonstrates a reproducible source for the columns, but it is a forensic reconstruction rather than proof that the original raw artifacts or extraction log were preserved.

1.4 CRU TS product, years, and grid-cell provenance

The temperature (tmp), precipitation (pre), and diurnal temperature range (dtr) variables in the analysis-ready data were extracted from CRU TS v4.09, a monthly gridded product at 0.5-degree resolution. The exact official archive release is cruts.2503051245.v4.09; the CRU TS v4.09 archive and variable-specific directories for temperature, precipitation, and diurnal temperature range are publicly accessible. CRU describes the product and its 0.5-degree monthly grid on its high-resolution climate-data page. The product should be cited as Harris et al. (2020), doi:10.1038/s41597-020-0453-3.

The actual study-year range used by tmp_cru_C_first_month, pre_cru_mm_first_month, and dtr_cru_C_first_month in the 441-record main model frame is 1982–2018; all three variables are complete over this range. It is not a 1901–2018 extraction. The reconstruction therefore reads the 1981–1990, 1991–2000, 2001–2010, and 2011–2020 archives, with 414 records occurring after 1990. Temperature and precipitation archives for 1961–1980 are additionally required only to calculate the 1961–1989 long baseline and the earliest rolling five-year baselines. These files were absent from the audited repository. The newly checked manifest identifies 16 required archives totalling 701,912,161 bytes (approximately 0.65 GiB) and records every official URL, byte size, and SHA-256 checksum.

The raw inputs can now be reobtained reproducibly, but they are still not committed to Git because of their size:

python scripts/download_cru_ts_v409.py

The downloader stores them under data_raw/cru_ts_4.09/, verifies both byte count and SHA-256 before reporting success, and refuses to replace an existing nonmatching file unless --force is supplied explicitly.

The original locality-to-cell lookup file was not retained. We therefore recovered the used grid cell empirically for each of 162 unique coordinates by comparing the stored first-month temperature and precipitation series with candidate CRU land cells within 100 km. Candidate cells were ranked first by the number of failures against the 0.01-degree-C/0.1-mm tolerances, then by scaled reconstruction error, and finally by distance. The selected cell reproduces all stored first-month values. Distance is the Haversine great-circle distance from the reported locality coordinates to the centre of the assigned 0.5-degree CRU grid cell, using an Earth radius of 6,371.0088 km. The recovered map is saved rather than recomputed during subsequent extraction. This step matters at coordinates lying exactly on a 0.5-degree cell boundary and at coastal localities, where a generic nearest-cell rule need not select the original land cell.

All 441 first-month values and all 426 available full-season values were reproduced to floating-point precision. This includes every one of the 414 post-1990 records. No reconstructed value differed by more than 0.001, and none crossed the prespecified hard thresholds of 0.01 degrees C for temperature/DTR or 0.1 mm for precipitation. The earlier failed within-season check used CRU TS v4.10 and an approximate grid lookup; it was a product-version and cell-mapping mismatch, not evidence that the post-1990 climate values lacked a source.

For a breeding window wholly within a calendar year, temperature and DTR are means across the named months and precipitation is their sum. For the 63 complete windows whose reported start month is later than the end month (for example, October–January), the historically stored full-season fields retained all named months under the record’s single climate_year: October–December and January were read from that same calendar year. Those fields and the corresponding same-year H1 fit are retained only as a provenance audit. In the final preregistered H1 analysis, the full-season means and within-season standard deviations were re-extracted from CRU TS v4.09 using the corrected calendar convention: months through December use climate_year, and January-side months use climate_year + 1. The product and recovered grid-cell mapping were first validated on 22 non-cross-year records from 1982–1990, with maximum absolute differences of \(4.0\times10^{-9}\) degrees C and \(4.8\times10^{-8}\) mm. This correction affects only full-season variables and H1; the main climate and anomaly models use the first breeding month and therefore require no refitting.

1.5 NOAA NDVI products and extraction scale

The Earth Engine script in ndvi_extraction/EPP_NDVI_NOAA_AVHRR_VIIRS_median_30x30km_first_full_records.js uses two daily NOAA Climate Data Record products:

  • NOAA Climate Data Record (CDR) of AVHRR Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), Version 5, Earth Engine collection NOAA/CDR/AVHRR/NDVI/V5 and NOAA dataset doi:10.7289/V5ZG6QH9. The Earth Engine collection covers 24 June 1981 through 31 December 2013. It supplies 399 final-model records dated 1982–2013.
  • NOAA Climate Data Record (CDR) of VIIRS Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), Version 1, Earth Engine collection NOAA/CDR/VIIRS/NDVI/V1 and NOAA dataset doi:10.25921/gakh-st76. The collection begins on 1 January 2014 and is continuing; it supplies the 42 final-model records dated 2014–2018.

Both products provide daily NDVI on the same 0.05-degree by 0.05-degree global grid. Earth Engine reports a nominal band pixel size of 5,566 m. Throughout this project, a pixel means one native 0.05-degree NOAA CDR grid cell at this nominal scale. It does not mean a native 375-m or 750-m VIIRS sensor observation, and it does not mean the complete 30-by-30-km extraction window. Because the source grid is geographic, 0.05 degrees is the defining spatial resolution and the physical east–west width of a grid cell decreases with latitude.

The script masks daily cells flagged as cloud, cloud shadow, water, sunglint, night, or invalid channel data and applies the catalog scale factor of 0.0001. For each record it constructs a square extending 15 km from the study coordinate in each direction. It then calculates the temporal median of valid daily NDVI separately for every native product pixel over the first breeding month or complete breeding window, followed by the arithmetic mean of those pixel-level medians across the 30-by-30-km square using scale = 5566. Consequently, despite the historical column names, ndvi_first_month_median_30km and ndvi_full_season_median_30km are temporal-pixel medians followed by a spatial mean; they are not spatial medians of the 30-km window.

1.5.1 NDVI missingness and spatial selectivity

Among the 571 response-complete records considered for the common environmental model frame, first-month NDVI was unavailable for 111. The extraction audit separated these cases without an unresolved remainder: 60 records could not be submitted with the required inputs because coordinates or the first breeding month were missing, while 51 had complete inputs but returned a null NDVI value because no valid pixel remained in the 30-by-30-km window after quality masking. The latter category is important because water, cloud, shadow, sunglint, night, and invalid-channel masks do not remove study systems at random.

At the species level, the complete-case restriction reduced the dataset from 153 to 110 Clements species. Thirty-three of these 43 lost species had no usable NDVI record: 17 lacked extraction inputs for every record, 15 returned no valid pixel for every record, and one had both failure mechanisms across its records. The other 10 species had at least one NDVI value and were removed by another model-frame requirement. Nine of the 15 species lost entirely after NDVI masking belonged to unequivocal seabird families; the masked set also included island and high-latitude populations. Consequently, the environmental models under-represent marine, island, and polar study systems. This is a limitation on their ecological scope of inference, not only a loss of sample size. Record- and species-level classifications are retained in ndvi_missingness_audit.csv and ndvi_species_loss_audit.csv.

The loss was also phylogenetically structured at the order level, rather than being dispersed evenly across the avian tree. Among Clements species, Suliformes declined from six represented species before environmental complete-case filtering to none in the final 441-record frame, Procellariiformes from nine to three, and Sphenisciformes from six to two. Thus, one order was removed completely and two marine orders lost most of their representation. This structural thinning is relevant to interpretation of the phylogenetic variance component and strengthens the limitation on inference to marine and polar lineages.

1.6 Climate-anomaly sign and time windows

Climate anomalies are stored as signed differences: the CRU value in the study year minus the corresponding local baseline, never as an absolute deviation. For climate variable \(x\), locality \(i\), breeding month or window \(m\), and study year \(y\),

\[ A^{\mathrm{short}}_{i,m,y}=x_{i,m,y}-\frac{1}{5}\sum_{t=y-5}^{y-1}x_{i,m,t}, \]

and

\[ A^{\mathrm{long}}_{i,m,y}=x_{i,m,y}-\frac{1}{29}\sum_{t=1961}^{1989}x_{i,m,t}. \]

Thus positive temperature anomalies denote warmer conditions and positive precipitation anomalies wetter conditions than the relevant local baseline. The primary anomaly models use only the first reported breeding-season month. A direct CRU TS v4.09 reconstruction reproduced every stored short- and long-baseline value and every signed anomaly for all 441 first-month records (and all 426 available full-season records), with no absolute difference greater than 0.001. Exact definitions, sign interpretation, standardization, and the independent algebraic identity check are also given in Section 6.2.

## CRU TS product, years, and grid-cell provenance

The temperature (`tmp`), precipitation (`pre`), and diurnal temperature range (`dtr`) variables in the analysis-ready data were extracted from **CRU TS v4.09**, a monthly gridded product at 0.5-degree resolution. The exact official archive release is `cruts.2503051245.v4.09`; the [CRU TS v4.09 archive](https://crudata.uea.ac.uk/cru/data/hrg/cru_ts_4.09/) and variable-specific directories for [temperature](https://crudata.uea.ac.uk/cru/data/hrg/cru_ts_4.09/cruts.2503051245.v4.09/tmp/), [precipitation](https://crudata.uea.ac.uk/cru/data/hrg/cru_ts_4.09/cruts.2503051245.v4.09/pre/), and [diurnal temperature range](https://crudata.uea.ac.uk/cru/data/hrg/cru_ts_4.09/cruts.2503051245.v4.09/dtr/) are publicly accessible. CRU describes the product and its 0.5-degree monthly grid on its [high-resolution climate-data page](https://crudata.uea.ac.uk/cru/data/hrg/). The product should be cited as Harris et al. (2020), [doi:10.1038/s41597-020-0453-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-020-0453-3).

The actual study-year range used by `tmp_cru_C_first_month`, `pre_cru_mm_first_month`, and `dtr_cru_C_first_month` in the 441-record main model frame is **1982--2018**; all three variables are complete over this range. It is not a 1901--2018 extraction. The reconstruction therefore reads the 1981--1990, 1991--2000, 2001--2010, and 2011--2020 archives, with 414 records occurring after 1990. Temperature and precipitation archives for 1961--1980 are additionally required only to calculate the 1961--1989 long baseline and the earliest rolling five-year baselines. These files were absent from the audited repository. The newly checked manifest identifies 16 required archives totalling 701,912,161 bytes (approximately 0.65 GiB) and records every official URL, byte size, and SHA-256 checksum.

The raw inputs can now be reobtained reproducibly, but they are still not committed to Git because of their size:

```powershell
python scripts/download_cru_ts_v409.py
```

The downloader stores them under `data_raw/cru_ts_4.09/`, verifies both byte count and SHA-256 before reporting success, and refuses to replace an existing nonmatching file unless `--force` is supplied explicitly.

The original locality-to-cell lookup file was not retained. We therefore recovered the used grid cell empirically for each of 162 unique coordinates by comparing the stored first-month temperature and precipitation series with candidate CRU land cells within 100 km. Candidate cells were ranked first by the number of failures against the 0.01-degree-C/0.1-mm tolerances, then by scaled reconstruction error, and finally by distance. The selected cell reproduces all stored first-month values. Distance is the Haversine great-circle distance from the reported locality coordinates to the centre of the assigned 0.5-degree CRU grid cell, using an Earth radius of 6,371.0088 km. The recovered map is saved rather than recomputed during subsequent extraction. This step matters at coordinates lying exactly on a 0.5-degree cell boundary and at coastal localities, where a generic nearest-cell rule need not select the original land cell.

## NOAA NDVI products and extraction scale

The Earth Engine script in `ndvi_extraction/EPP_NDVI_NOAA_AVHRR_VIIRS_median_30x30km_first_full_records.js` uses two daily NOAA Climate Data Record products:

- **NOAA Climate Data Record (CDR) of AVHRR Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), Version 5**, Earth Engine collection [`NOAA/CDR/AVHRR/NDVI/V5`](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NOAA_CDR_AVHRR_NDVI_V5) and NOAA dataset [doi:10.7289/V5ZG6QH9](https://doi.org/10.7289/V5ZG6QH9). The Earth Engine collection covers 24 June 1981 through 31 December 2013. It supplies 399 final-model records dated 1982--2013.
- **NOAA Climate Data Record (CDR) of VIIRS Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), Version 1**, Earth Engine collection [`NOAA/CDR/VIIRS/NDVI/V1`](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NOAA_CDR_VIIRS_NDVI_V1) and NOAA dataset [doi:10.25921/gakh-st76](https://doi.org/10.25921/gakh-st76). The collection begins on 1 January 2014 and is continuing; it supplies the 42 final-model records dated 2014--2018.

Both products provide daily NDVI on the same **0.05-degree by 0.05-degree global grid**. Earth Engine reports a nominal band pixel size of **5,566 m**. Throughout this project, a *pixel* means one native 0.05-degree NOAA CDR grid cell at this nominal scale. It does not mean a native 375-m or 750-m VIIRS sensor observation, and it does not mean the complete 30-by-30-km extraction window. Because the source grid is geographic, 0.05 degrees is the defining spatial resolution and the physical east--west width of a grid cell decreases with latitude.

The script masks daily cells flagged as cloud, cloud shadow, water, sunglint, night, or invalid channel data and applies the catalog scale factor of 0.0001. For each record it constructs a square extending 15 km from the study coordinate in each direction. It then calculates the temporal median of valid daily NDVI **separately for every native product pixel** over the first breeding month or complete breeding window, followed by the arithmetic mean of those pixel-level medians across the 30-by-30-km square using `scale = 5566`. Consequently, despite the historical column names, `ndvi_first_month_median_30km` and `ndvi_full_season_median_30km` are temporal-pixel medians followed by a spatial mean; they are not spatial medians of the 30-km window.

### NDVI missingness and spatial selectivity

Among the 571 response-complete records considered for the common environmental
model frame, first-month NDVI was unavailable for 111. The extraction audit
separated these cases without an unresolved remainder: 60 records could not be
submitted with the required inputs because coordinates or the first breeding
month were missing, while 51 had complete inputs but returned a null NDVI value
because no valid pixel remained in the 30-by-30-km window after quality
masking. The latter category is important because water, cloud, shadow,
sunglint, night, and invalid-channel masks do not remove study systems at
random.

At the species level, the complete-case restriction reduced the dataset from
153 to 110 Clements species. Thirty-three of these 43 lost species had no
usable NDVI record: 17 lacked extraction inputs for every record, 15 returned
no valid pixel for every record, and one had both failure mechanisms across
its records. The other 10 species had at least one NDVI value and were removed
by another model-frame requirement. Nine of the 15 species lost entirely after
NDVI masking belonged to unequivocal seabird families; the masked set also
included island and high-latitude populations. Consequently, the environmental
models under-represent marine, island, and polar study systems. This is a
limitation on their ecological scope of inference, not only a loss of sample
size. Record- and species-level classifications are retained in
[`ndvi_missingness_audit.csv`](data/processed/ndvi_missingness_audit.csv) and
[`ndvi_species_loss_audit.csv`](data/processed/ndvi_species_loss_audit.csv).

The loss was also **phylogenetically structured at the order level**, rather
than being dispersed evenly across the avian tree. Among Clements species,
Suliformes declined from six represented species before environmental
complete-case filtering to none in the final 441-record frame,
Procellariiformes from nine to three, and Sphenisciformes from six to two.
Thus, one order was removed completely and two marine orders lost most of
their representation. This structural thinning is relevant to interpretation
of the phylogenetic variance component and strengthens the limitation on
inference to marine and polar lineages.

## Climate-anomaly sign and time windows

Climate anomalies are stored as signed differences: the CRU value in the study year minus the corresponding local baseline, never as an absolute deviation. For climate variable $x$, locality $i$, breeding month or window $m$, and study year $y$,

$$
A^{\mathrm{short}}_{i,m,y}=x_{i,m,y}-\frac{1}{5}\sum_{t=y-5}^{y-1}x_{i,m,t},
$$

and

$$
A^{\mathrm{long}}_{i,m,y}=x_{i,m,y}-\frac{1}{29}\sum_{t=1961}^{1989}x_{i,m,t}.
$$

Thus positive temperature anomalies denote warmer conditions and positive precipitation anomalies wetter conditions than the relevant local baseline. The primary anomaly models use only the first reported breeding-season month. A direct CRU TS v4.09 reconstruction reproduced every stored short- and long-baseline value and every signed anomaly for all 441 first-month records (and all 426 available full-season records), with no absolute difference greater than 0.001. Exact definitions, sign interpretation, standardization, and the independent algebraic identity check are also given in @sec-climate-anomaly-definitions.
