6.1 Overview

This chapter defines the final primary environmental model set. The primary model set was organised around three environmental hypotheses.

The preregistration specified direct environmental and geographic predictors rather than PCA axes. Because those predictors are strongly collinear, the PCA specification was added after registration and is reported as the primary multivariate analysis. The registered direct-predictor specification is reported in the following chapter and is used to assess sensitivity to this dimensional-reduction decision.

  • The PCA models tested whether broad environmental-geographic gradients were associated with EPP.

  • The short-anomaly models tested whether signed recent deviations from local climatic conditions, measured relative to the five years preceding the breeding record, were associated with EPP.

  • The long-anomaly models tested whether signed deviations from the 1961-1989 climatic baseline were associated with EPP.

Each hypothesis group is presented as a mean-only model, allowing environmental predictors and methodological controls to affect the expected EPP rate while using a single global precision parameter (phi ~ 1). Methodological controls are nest box and marker type. All models use the same random-effect structure for population, non-phylogenetic species identity, and phylogenetic species covariance. Exploratory location-scale versions of these models are presented in a separate chapter.

6.2 Climate anomaly definitions

The anomaly models use the CRU value for one calendar month only: the first reported breeding-season month (climate_month) in the study year (climate_year). They do not average climate over the full breeding window. At locality \(\ell\), in study year \(y\) and first breeding month \(m\), let \(X_{\ell,y,m}\) denote either monthly mean temperature (degrees C) or monthly total precipitation (mm). The short-term baseline is the mean for the same locality and calendar month over the five years immediately preceding the study year:

\[ \bar X^{\mathrm{short}}_{\ell,y,m} = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{k=y-5}^{y-1} X_{\ell,k,m}, \qquad A^{\mathrm{short}}_{\ell,y,m} = X_{\ell,y,m}-\bar X^{\mathrm{short}}_{\ell,y,m}. \]

The long-term baseline is the mean for the same locality and calendar month over 1961–1989:

\[ \bar X^{\mathrm{long}}_{\ell,m} = \frac{1}{29}\sum_{k=1961}^{1989} X_{\ell,k,m}, \qquad A^{\mathrm{long}}_{\ell,y,m} = X_{\ell,y,m}-\bar X^{\mathrm{long}}_{\ell,m}. \]

Thus, all four modelled anomalies are signed differences, not absolute deviations. A positive temperature anomaly means that the first breeding month was warmer than its local baseline and a negative value means that it was cooler. A positive precipitation anomaly means wetter conditions and a negative value means drier conditions. The corresponding full-season variables in the dataset were constructed by the same signed subtraction across the full breeding window, but they are not used in these two primary anomaly models.

After complete-case filtering, each signed anomaly was standardized within the common 441-record model frame as

\[ A_z = \frac{A-\bar A_{\mathcal D}}{s_{\mathcal D}(A)}, \]

where \(\mathcal D\) is the main-model data frame. No absolute-value transformation was applied before or after standardization. Consequently, a positive regression coefficient represents increasing EPP as the anomaly becomes more positive (warmer or wetter relative to baseline); it does not represent an association with the magnitude of climatic departure irrespective of direction. Models using \(|A|\) would test a different hypothesis and were not fitted here, so their interpretation is not interchangeable with that of the earlier absolute-deviation analysis.

The following executable audit reconstructs every stored anomaly in the model frame from its study-year value and baseline. The presence of both negative and positive values, together with negligible reconstruction error for \(A=X-\bar X\), verifies the signed definition rather than relying on the column names.

6.8 Beta-binomial precision (\(\phi\))

The beta-binomial precision parameter quantifies residual extra-binomial heterogeneity after conditioning on the fixed effects and the three random-intercept components. Larger \(\phi\) means less extra-binomial variation, with the ordinary binomial approached as \(\phi\) increases. The corresponding within-record correlation among Bernoulli trials is \(\rho = 1/(\phi + 1)\).

## Overview

This chapter defines the final primary environmental model set. The primary model set was organised around three environmental hypotheses.

The preregistration specified direct environmental and geographic predictors rather than PCA axes. Because those predictors are strongly collinear, the PCA specification was added after registration and is reported as the primary multivariate analysis. The registered direct-predictor specification is reported in the following chapter and is used to assess sensitivity to this dimensional-reduction decision.

- The PCA models tested whether broad environmental-geographic gradients were associated with EPP.

- The short-anomaly models tested whether signed recent deviations from local climatic conditions, measured relative to the five years preceding the breeding record, were associated with EPP.

- The long-anomaly models tested whether signed deviations from the 1961-1989 climatic baseline were associated with EPP.

Each hypothesis group is presented as a mean-only model, allowing environmental predictors and methodological controls to affect the expected EPP rate while using a single global precision parameter (`phi ~ 1`). Methodological controls are nest box and marker type. All models use the same random-effect structure for population, non-phylogenetic species identity, and phylogenetic species covariance. Exploratory location-scale versions of these models are presented in a separate chapter.

## Climate anomaly definitions {#sec-climate-anomaly-definitions}

The anomaly models use the CRU value for **one calendar month only**: the first reported breeding-season month (`climate_month`) in the study year (`climate_year`). They do not average climate over the full breeding window. At locality $\ell$, in study year $y$ and first breeding month $m$, let $X_{\ell,y,m}$ denote either monthly mean temperature (degrees C) or monthly total precipitation (mm). The short-term baseline is the mean for the same locality and calendar month over the five years immediately preceding the study year:

$$
\bar X^{\mathrm{short}}_{\ell,y,m}
= \frac{1}{5}\sum_{k=y-5}^{y-1} X_{\ell,k,m},
\qquad
A^{\mathrm{short}}_{\ell,y,m}
= X_{\ell,y,m}-\bar X^{\mathrm{short}}_{\ell,y,m}.
$$

The long-term baseline is the mean for the same locality and calendar month over 1961--1989:

$$
\bar X^{\mathrm{long}}_{\ell,m}
= \frac{1}{29}\sum_{k=1961}^{1989} X_{\ell,k,m},
\qquad
A^{\mathrm{long}}_{\ell,y,m}
= X_{\ell,y,m}-\bar X^{\mathrm{long}}_{\ell,m}.
$$

Thus, all four modelled anomalies are **signed differences, not absolute deviations**. A positive temperature anomaly means that the first breeding month was warmer than its local baseline and a negative value means that it was cooler. A positive precipitation anomaly means wetter conditions and a negative value means drier conditions. The corresponding full-season variables in the dataset were constructed by the same signed subtraction across the full breeding window, but they are not used in these two primary anomaly models.

After complete-case filtering, each signed anomaly was standardized within the common 441-record model frame as

$$
A_z = \frac{A-\bar A_{\mathcal D}}{s_{\mathcal D}(A)},
$$

where $\mathcal D$ is the main-model data frame. No absolute-value transformation was applied before or after standardization. Consequently, a positive regression coefficient represents increasing EPP as the anomaly becomes more positive (warmer or wetter relative to baseline); it does **not** represent an association with the magnitude of climatic departure irrespective of direction. Models using $|A|$ would test a different hypothesis and were not fitted here, so their interpretation is not interchangeable with that of the earlier absolute-deviation analysis.

The following executable audit reconstructs every stored anomaly in the model frame from its study-year value and baseline. The presence of both negative and positive values, together with negligible reconstruction error for $A=X-\bar X$, verifies the signed definition rather than relying on the column names.

## Beta-binomial precision ($\phi$)

The beta-binomial precision parameter quantifies residual extra-binomial heterogeneity after conditioning on the fixed effects and the three random-intercept components. Larger $\phi$ means less extra-binomial variation, with the ordinary binomial approached as $\phi$ increases. The corresponding within-record correlation among Bernoulli trials is $\rho = 1/(\phi + 1)$.

```{r}
#| label: main-env-phi
```
