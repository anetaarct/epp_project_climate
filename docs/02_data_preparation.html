2.1 Overview

This chapter documents the preparation of the analytical dataset used in the analyses. The workflow starts from the analysis input file, checks the response variables, restricts the data to socially monogamous species, defines population-level identifiers, and summarises the final dataset used in the Bayesian models.

The legacy database of Brouwer and Griffith contained 488 unique publications. Of these, 286 publications (58.6%) were excluded because their EPP estimates could not be assigned to individual study years, leaving 202 publications (41.4%) with annual-resolution EPP data. Results of the literature update for studies published after 1 August 2017, including the number contributing eligible year-specific EPP data, are reported separately in the PRISMA flow diagram. Throughout this chapter, study counts refer to unique publications (id_reference), species counts use Clements v2025 names, and repeated population-year observations are not counted as separate studies.

2.4 Publication-overlap resolution

To prevent repeated use of the same population-years across publications, records were compared using the Clements-matched species name (Clements_name), study year, and geographic coordinates. Two publication series were treated as potentially overlapping when they represented the same Clements species, shared at least one study year, and their reported locations were no more than 25 km apart. Within every such comparison, the publication series covering the longest temporal span was retained and the shorter series was removed in full, including its non-overlapping years. This prevents a shorter report derived from the same study system from contributing additional weight beside a longer report. Exact repeated biological results within the same publication were reduced to one record before the between-publication comparison.

When temporal spans were equal, ties were resolved reproducibly using, in order, the number of observed years, the total number of sampled broods, the number of records, and the reference identifier. The rule was applied to all species before restricting the dataset by breeding system. No record was removed solely because two studies were geographically close: an overlapping study year was also required.

2.8 Breeding system restriction

The main analyses are restricted to socially monogamous species. This restriction is important because EPP is defined relative to social pair bonds. EPP estimates are therefore not directly comparable across systems without stable social pair bonds, or across polyandrous, lekking, or non-pair-bonded mating systems.

After resolving duplicate and overlapping publication series, the combined annual dataset contained 237 unique publications representing 167 species. The breeding-system restriction excluded 14 publications representing 13 species: 13 publications inherited from the Brouwer and Griffith database and one publication identified in the literature update. Twelve species were removed from the analysis entirely; one additional species, the Aquatic Warbler (Acrocephalus paludicola), also had a retained publication classified as socially monogamous. The socially monogamous dataset therefore contained 223 unique publications representing 155 species. These counts are publication- and species-level totals, not counts of annual records.

2.9 Response variable checks

The response variable is the number of broods with EPP out of the total number of broods sampled. Records were retained for modelling only when brood counts were available, the total number of sampled broods was greater than zero, and the number of EPP broods did not exceed the total number of sampled broods.

2.12 Environmental predictor complete-case restriction

The response-complete dataset is broader than the common frame used by the main environmental models. The model-fitting script additionally requires complete first-month climate and NDVI values, geographic covariates, anomaly variables, categorical moderators, and phylogenetic identifiers. The following audit names this previously implicit filtering step and separates missing NDVI caused by unavailable extraction inputs from null Earth Engine results after quality masking.

## Publication-overlap resolution

To prevent repeated use of the same population-years across publications, records were compared using the Clements-matched species name (`Clements_name`), study year, and geographic coordinates. Two publication series were treated as potentially overlapping when they represented the same Clements species, shared at least one study year, and their reported locations were no more than 25 km apart. Within every such comparison, the publication series covering the longest temporal span was retained and the shorter series was removed in full, including its non-overlapping years. This prevents a shorter report derived from the same study system from contributing additional weight beside a longer report. Exact repeated biological results within the same publication were reduced to one record before the between-publication comparison.

When temporal spans were equal, ties were resolved reproducibly using, in order, the number of observed years, the total number of sampled broods, the number of records, and the reference identifier. The rule was applied to all species before restricting the dataset by breeding system. No record was removed solely because two studies were geographically close: an overlapping study year was also required.

The main analyses are restricted to socially monogamous species. This restriction is important because EPP is defined relative to social pair bonds. EPP estimates are therefore not directly comparable across systems without stable social pair bonds, or across polyandrous, lekking, or non-pair-bonded mating systems.

After resolving duplicate and overlapping publication series, the combined annual dataset contained 237 unique publications representing 167 species. The breeding-system restriction excluded 14 publications representing 13 species: 13 publications inherited from the Brouwer and Griffith database and one publication identified in the literature update. Twelve species were removed from the analysis entirely; one additional species, the Aquatic Warbler (*Acrocephalus paludicola*), also had a retained publication classified as socially monogamous. The socially monogamous dataset therefore contained 223 unique publications representing 155 species. These counts are publication- and species-level totals, not counts of annual records.

The response variable is the number of broods with EPP out of the total number of broods sampled. Records were retained for modelling only when brood counts were available, the total number of sampled broods was greater than zero, and the number of EPP broods did not exceed the total number of sampled broods.

  "recomputed_pre_cru_mm_full_season",
  "recomputed_dtr_cru_C_full_season"
)
missing_corrected_columns <- setdiff(
  required_corrected_columns,
  names(corrected_fullseason_climate)
)
if (length(missing_corrected_columns)) {
  stop(
    "Corrected full-season climate audit is missing: ",
    paste(missing_corrected_columns, collapse = ", ")
  )
}

environment_window_data <- main_environmental_frame %>%
  mutate(epp_row_id = as.character(epp_row_id)) %>%
  left_join(
    corrected_fullseason_climate %>%
      transmute(
        epp_row_id = as.character(epp_row_id),
        crosses_year_boundary =
          tolower(as.character(crosses_year_boundary)) == "true",
        tmp_cru_C_full_season_corrected =
          as.numeric(recomputed_tmp_cru_C_full_season),
        pre_cru_mm_full_season_corrected =
          as.numeric(recomputed_pre_cru_mm_full_season),
        dtr_cru_C_full_season_corrected =
          as.numeric(recomputed_dtr_cru_C_full_season)
      ),
    by = "epp_row_id",
    relationship = "one-to-one"
  )

if (nrow(environment_window_data) != 441L ||
    sum(environment_window_data$crosses_year_boundary %in% TRUE, na.rm = TRUE) != 63L) {
  stop("Corrected environmental-window audit did not reproduce 441 records and 63 cross-year seasons.")
}

environment_window_pairs <- tribble(
  ~variable, ~first_month, ~full_season,
  "Temperature", "tmp_cru_C_first_month", "tmp_cru_C_full_season_corrected",
  "Precipitation", "pre_cru_mm_first_month", "pre_cru_mm_full_season_corrected",
  "DTR", "dtr_cru_C_first_month", "dtr_cru_C_full_season_corrected",
  "NDVI", "ndvi_first_month_median_30km", "ndvi_full_season_median_30km"
)

environment_window_correlations <- purrr::pmap_dfr(
  environment_window_pairs,
  function(variable, first_month, full_season) {
    keep <- complete.cases(environment_window_data[, c(first_month, full_season)])
    test <- cor.test(
      environment_window_data[[first_month]][keep],
      environment_window_data[[full_season]][keep],
      method = "pearson"
    )

    tibble(
      variable = variable,
      n_complete = sum(keep),
      pearson_r = unname(test$estimate),
      lower_95_ci = test$conf.int[1],
      upper_95_ci = test$conf.int[2]
    )
  }
)

write_csv_safe(
  environment_window_correlations,
  "models/climate_provenance/corrected_environment_window_correlations.csv"
)

After assigning January-side CRU months to the following calendar year for all 63 cross-year seasons, the correspondence between first-month and full-season values was high for temperature (*r* = `r sprintf("%.3f", environment_window_correlations$pearson_r[environment_window_correlations$variable == "Temperature"])`), DTR (*r* = `r sprintf("%.3f", environment_window_correlations$pearson_r[environment_window_correlations$variable == "DTR"])`), and NDVI (*r* = `r sprintf("%.3f", environment_window_correlations$pearson_r[environment_window_correlations$variable == "NDVI"])`), but only moderate for precipitation (*r* = `r sprintf("%.3f", environment_window_correlations$pearson_r[environment_window_correlations$variable == "Precipitation"])`). These correlations are calculated on the 441-record main environmental frame using the corrected CRU TS v4.09 full-season fields; pair-specific sample sizes are reported in the table. The two temporal windows should therefore not be regarded as interchangeable for precipitation: precipitation coefficients in the main models describe conditions in the first breeding-season month and should be interpreted with this limitation. The preregistered analysis specified environmental aggregation across the complete breeding window; using first-month predictors in the main models is a deviation from that specification and is documented here for the Supplementary Methods.
