11.1 Overview

Botero and Rubenstein (2012) analysed the proportion of nests containing extra-pair young, the same response definition used here, but aggregated the data to one value per species. Their only retained climate association for infidelity was a weak positive effect of within-year temperature variance (\(\beta = 0.042\), \(P = 0.041\)) in a final model with \(R^2 = 0.08\). This chapter tests that predictor directly in the present population-resolved, beta-binomial phylogenetic framework and then reproduces their species-level PGLS workflow on our data. The comparison is framed as a test of generalisability, not as a claim that the earlier result was strong.

WarningScope of the comparison: context, not a formal replication

Only 44 of the 122 species in B&R’s infidelity dataset occur in the present annual-data model set. This limited overlap is driven primarily by our requirement for year-resolved population records, not by failed taxonomy: all 122 B&R names were resolved to Clements v2025. In addition, adult mortality and coloniality, which entered B&R’s candidate model, are not available consistently in our dataset. Our models instead retain the pre-specified phylogenetic, population, marker-type, and nest-box controls.

Consequently, the analyses in this chapter provide comparative context and a test of generalisability, not a formal replication of B&R’s complete analysis. The separate Table S1 check below validates the PGLS implementation on their original response and predictors; it does not remove the differences in species coverage, covariates, or phylogeny.

11.2 Climate reconstruction

Monthly CRU TS v4.09 temperature and precipitation were extracted at the audited 0.5-degree locality cells. The locality-to-cell assignments were held fixed while changing the CRU product version, so the v4.09–v4.10 comparison does not conflate product version with spatial remapping. All populations use the fixed 1961–1990 reference window, preventing the derived climate descriptors from being confounded with study year. No climate value was imputed. Sites without a reported breeding-season end month retain the two within-year metrics but are excluded from analyses requiring among-year breeding-season variance.

Breeding seasons that cross December/January are treated as one continuous season: months from the reported start through December use reference year \(y\), whereas January-side months use \(y+1\). Thus, a season beginning in 1990 may use calendar months from 1991. The v4.09 extraction consequently includes the 1991–2000 archives solely to complete the 1961–1990 reference seasons. All 23 unique cross-year locality–season combinations (63 population-year records) retained 30 complete temperature and precipitation seasons.

For variable \(x\) in year \(y\), within-year variance is \(s_y^2 = \operatorname{Var}(x_{y,1}, \ldots, x_{y,12})\); the locality value is the mean of \(s_y^2\) across 1961–1990. Among-year variance is the variance, across those 30 years, of the mean over the reported breeding-season months. The four variables are:

Variable Definition
br_tmp_withinyear_var Mean annual variance of the 12 monthly temperatures
br_pre_withinyear_var Mean annual variance of the 12 monthly precipitation totals
br_tmp_amongyear_var Variance among annual breeding-season mean temperatures
br_pre_amongyear_var Variance among annual breeding-season mean precipitation totals

Each predictor is standardised only after complete-case filtering of its own model frame. DTR is not included in the comparison models: daily thermal amplitude and B&R’s seasonal monthly variance are different quantities.

11.4 Validation of the PGLS pipeline on B&R Table S1

Before applying the species-level PGLS workflow to our data, we tested it on the 122 original infidelity rows in B&R Table S1. The source workbook is accepted only if its SHA-256 checksum and row count match the recorded source manifest. Following the reported final infidelity model, the response is the arcsine-square-root transformation of the proportion of broods with EPP; adult mortality and within-year temperature variance are standardised; and a PGLS with Pagel’s lambda is fitted. This checksum-verified validation uses B&R’s published Table S1 values and is therefore invariant to our CRU product version; its existing validated fit is retained rather than needlessly refitted.

11.5 Bayesian comparison models

The priors are reproduced literally from the main fitting script and compared programmatically with its parsed priors_mu assignment before fitting. A difference stops the script. All three models otherwise use the main model’s beta-binomial family, sampler settings, population random intercept, non-phylogenetic species intercept, phylogenetic covariance, nest-box effect, and marker-type effect. Coloniality and adult mortality were unavailable and were not replaced by new proxies.

11.5.2 B1b: retained B&R predictor adjusted for latitude

B1 is deliberately left without latitude because it is the direct, head-to-head test of B&R’s retained seasonality predictor. B1b is a separate confounding test: it adds standardised absolute latitude to the otherwise identical model. This distinction matters because within-year temperature variance and absolute latitude are appreciably associated in these data (\(r = 0.400\)), precisely the concern raised for this literature by Brouwer and Griffith (2019).

11.6 C1: Species-level PGLS on our data

The model contains within-year temperature and precipitation variance and uses the same Clements-matched phylogeny. This is a supplementary decomposition of the discrepancy, not a preferred primary model.

11.8 Interpretation rule

The head-to-head conclusion is based on B1; B1b must not replace it. B1b asks whether the seasonality coefficient contains information independent of absolute latitude. If the coefficient is attenuated or loses posterior support after adding latitude, while latitude itself remains unsupported in the main latitude sensitivity analysis and in B1b, the B&R association is statistically indistinguishable from a null latitudinal gradient. That is a separate result, not evidence that latitude explains EPP.

If B1’s 95% credible interval for within-year temperature variance includes zero and the species-level PGLS is also null, the discrepancy is attributed primarily to data and sampling rather than to species averaging or the arcsine transformation. B&R’s among-year breeding-season variance was already absent from their final infidelity model; our year-specific anomaly results therefore extend, rather than contradict, that null. Any manuscript statement should retain the modest magnitude and borderline support of the original result.

## Overview

Botero and Rubenstein (2012) analysed the proportion of nests containing
extra-pair young, the same response definition used here, but aggregated the
data to one value per species. Their only retained climate association for
infidelity was a weak positive effect of within-year temperature variance
($\beta = 0.042$, $P = 0.041$) in a final model with $R^2 = 0.08$. This chapter
tests that predictor directly in the present population-resolved,
beta-binomial phylogenetic framework and then reproduces their species-level
PGLS workflow on our data. The comparison is framed as a test of
generalisability, not as a claim that the earlier result was strong.

::: {.callout-warning title="Scope of the comparison: context, not a formal replication"}
Only **44 of the 122 species** in B&R's infidelity dataset occur in the
present annual-data model set. This limited overlap is driven primarily by
our requirement for year-resolved population records, not by failed taxonomy:
all 122 B&R names were resolved to Clements v2025. In addition, adult
mortality and coloniality, which entered B&R's candidate model, are not
available consistently in our dataset. Our models instead retain the
pre-specified phylogenetic, population, marker-type, and nest-box controls.

Consequently, the analyses in this chapter provide **comparative context and
a test of generalisability**, not a formal replication of B&R's complete
analysis. The separate Table S1 check below validates the PGLS implementation
on their original response and predictors; it does not remove the differences
in species coverage, covariates, or phylogeny.
:::

## Climate reconstruction

Monthly CRU TS v4.09 temperature and precipitation were extracted at the
audited 0.5-degree locality cells. The locality-to-cell assignments were held
fixed while changing the CRU product version, so the v4.09--v4.10 comparison
does not conflate product version with spatial remapping. All populations use
the fixed 1961--1990 reference window, preventing the derived climate
descriptors from being confounded with study year. No climate value was
imputed. Sites without a reported breeding-season end month retain the two
within-year metrics but are excluded from analyses requiring among-year
breeding-season variance.

Breeding seasons that cross December/January are treated as one continuous
season: months from the reported start through December use reference year
$y$, whereas January-side months use $y+1$. Thus, a season beginning in 1990
may use calendar months from 1991. The v4.09 extraction consequently includes
the 1991--2000 archives solely to complete the 1961--1990 reference seasons.
All 23 unique cross-year locality--season combinations (63 population-year
records) retained 30 complete temperature and precipitation seasons.

For variable $x$ in year $y$, within-year variance is
$s_y^2 = \operatorname{Var}(x_{y,1}, \ldots, x_{y,12})$; the locality value is
the mean of $s_y^2$ across 1961--1990. Among-year variance is the variance,
across those 30 years, of the mean over the reported breeding-season months.
The four variables are:

| Variable | Definition |
|---|---|
| `br_tmp_withinyear_var` | Mean annual variance of the 12 monthly temperatures |
| `br_pre_withinyear_var` | Mean annual variance of the 12 monthly precipitation totals |
| `br_tmp_amongyear_var` | Variance among annual breeding-season mean temperatures |
| `br_pre_amongyear_var` | Variance among annual breeding-season mean precipitation totals |

Each predictor is standardised only after complete-case filtering of its own
model frame. DTR is not included in the comparison models: daily thermal
amplitude and B&R's seasonal monthly variance are different quantities.

zero and its v4.09 estimate differs from the v4.10 estimate by $-0.001$; the
threshold crossing is therefore not a material product-version effect. With
four correlated climate predictors and no backward elimination, it is treated
as a weak secondary signal rather than a selected independent effect.

precipitation variance and uses the same Clements-matched phylogeny. This is a
